FileHop Privacy Policy

Cobaltline Software · Effective October 21, 2026

FileHop moves files and text between your phone and your Windows PC over your local network. The phone opens a web page served by FileHop on your PC; nothing is installed on the phone. This policy explains what the app does with data. The short version: FileHop does not collect, store or transmit any personal information to us or to anyone else, and your files only travel between your own devices.

What the app does not do

  • No account or sign-in.
  • No analytics, telemetry, advertising or tracking.
  • No cloud. Files and text go directly from one device to the other on your local network (or over the PC's own Wi-Fi when you use the direct connection). They never pass through our servers or the internet.
  • No access from outside your network. FileHop only answers devices with local network addresses, and only after they scanned the current QR code or typed its code. Phones only see what you send them; they can never browse your PC.

Data that stays on your PC

  • Files and text you receive are saved in the folder you choose (Downloads\FileHop by default). Unfinished transfers are kept in a hidden ".FileHop-partial" folder inside it so they can resume, and are removed when they finish or are cancelled.
  • History. To show your conversations and History, the app records for each transfer the file names, sizes, times, direction, the device involved and where the file was saved, and the text and links that were sent. Each phone's menu lets you clear its conversation or forget the phone, and uninstalling the app removes all of it.
  • Devices. For each phone, tablet or computer that connected, the app remembers a random device ID it created, the name shown in the app (a device type such as "iPhone", or the name you typed on the phone) and the browser type. Choose "Forget this phone" to remove one.
  • Settings (receive folder, theme, language and your choices in Settings) are saved in the app's local data folder.
  • Logs. If something goes wrong, a short technical log is written to the local data folder. It is never sent automatically; you can open the folder from Settings if you choose to share it with support.

Data on the phone

  • The phone page keeps a random device ID, the name you gave the phone (if any) and a note of unfinished uploads in the browser's local storage, so the PC recognizes the phone and an interrupted upload can resume. Clearing the browser's website data removes them.
  • While connected, the page holds a session cookie that only works with your PC, ends when you disconnect, quit FileHop or after two hours without use, and is never shared with any website.
  • Files the PC sends are saved on the phone only when you tap Save (or save a photo yourself).

Direct connection

When you choose the direct connection, FileHop turns on the Windows mobile hotspot with the network name and password already set in Windows, and shows them as a QR code on your PC's screen so the phone can join. FileHop does not change or store them, and turns the hotspot off again when you stop the direct connection or quit the app.

Network permissions

The app installs a Windows Firewall rule that lets devices on your local network reach FileHop's page (TCP ports 52720 to 52729). The rule only applies to FileHop and is removed when you uninstall the app. If you use "Fix it" in the connection help, Windows asks for your permission and adds a rule named "FileHop" for the same ports; Windows keeps that one until you remove it in Windows Defender Firewall settings.

Purchases

FileHop is a free download. Unlimited file size is a one-time in-app purchase handled entirely by the Microsoft Store. The app only asks the Microsoft Store whether the purchase exists on your account; it never sees your payment details. Microsoft's privacy statement applies to the purchase itself.

Children

The app does not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.
